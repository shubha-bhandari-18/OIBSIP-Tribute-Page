About Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam was an Indian aerospace scientist who played an important role in India's space and defence programmes. His scientific career was closely connected with organisations such as ISRO and DRDO.

Born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Kalam came from a modest background. Through education, determination and hard work, he built a remarkable career in science and technology.

Kalam became the 11th President of India in 2002 and served until 2007. He was widely admired for his simple lifestyle, humility and strong connection with students and young people.

Beyond his scientific and public career, Kalam inspired millions of students to think creatively, work with discipline and contribute to the development of the nation. His life continues to be remembered as an example of knowledge, perseverance and service.

His Legacy

Dr. Kalam's greatest legacy is the inspiration he gave to young people. He encouraged students to develop knowledge, confidence and a vision for the future.

His journey from Rameswaram to the highest constitutional office in India demonstrated how education, perseverance and dedication can create meaningful change.
